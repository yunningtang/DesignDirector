## Default looks to avoid — v4  <!-- rule:defaults-block -->

Pages built by models cluster around a few looks whatever the subject. Each item below is a **combination**; any single ingredient on its own (a serif headline, square corners, a grey background, a rounded button) is fine. Where the brief leaves the look open, do not land on one of these combinations. A combination is not a hit when the contract traces it to the subject: a hue named in HUE with its source, a face chosen for a stated reason, labels that mark a real sequence.

1. **Warm paper.** A cream or beige page (around #F4F1EA) with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta, rust or clay accent. For a warm, bookish, calm or crafted subject, treat the first palette that comes to mind as already spent.
2. **Neon night.** A near-black page with one acid-green, neon or vermilion accent and glowing edges.
3. **Broadsheet as decoration.** Hairline rules everywhere, plus zero radius, plus dense newspaper columns, plus an italic serif display, on a subject that is not a publication.
4. **Neutral app shell.** (A white or black page whose colour comes from the content, with square imagery and few boxes, is how most real sites look and is not this.) A pale cool-grey or white page (around #E9EDF1–#F7F8FA) where colour appears only as one accent on buttons and one headline word, with white large-radius cards and pill buttons on every section. Saturated colour, a tinted ground or a dark ground are all open to you.
5. **Poster headline.** A heavy grotesk headline set in ALL CAPS or very tight, often Archivo or Archivo Black, filling the hero, with a cobalt or navy accent and a white or pale ground. Let the headline get its character from the assigned faces and from the direction instead.
6. **The emphasised word.** On operate, read and transact pages: a headline where one word or phrase is switched to italic, or to the accent colour, or to a second typeface, to add personality. (On persuade and experience pages one emphasised word in the opening headline is allowed.) Give the headline character through size, weight, width, case, setting or layout instead.
7. **One corner habit.** Every card, button, input and image given the same treatment by habit: all pills and 16px+ cards, or all square boxes with 1–2px borders. Choose corners per role (controls, containers, media) and let at least two roles differ.
8. **Template chrome.** A small tracked-out ALL-CAPS label above every heading, "01 / 02 / 03" markers on content that is not a sequence, meta strings joined with middle dots, monospace for small labels that are not code or data, a thick coloured stripe down one side of cards, hard offset shadows (4px 4px 0) outside a world that actually calls for them, and a row of identical icon + heading + text cards.
9. **Fashionable effects.** A purple-to-blue or purple-to-pink gradient hero; gradient-filled text; glass panels (backdrop-filter blur) as decoration; glowing coloured halos; a fine grid or line-field background on a page that is not a map, plan or measurement surface; emoji used as icons or section markers. (Never, unless the brief names it.)
10. **Habit typefaces.** A face chosen because it came to mind first, with no reason in the contract. Faces that come to mind first for models: Fraunces, Newsreader, Cormorant, Instrument Serif, Playfair Display, IBM Plex, Space Grotesk, Inter, Bricolage Grotesque, Figtree. Any of them is fine when FORM says why it fits this subject; the hit is the habit, not the face.
11. **Stock top bar.** (V4.7: measured on 491 real headers.) A 64px white bar with a 1px grey line under it, a generic app icon in a rounded square next to the plain-text name, a grey tagline after the name ("Name  Short description"), 3-5 links on the right, an outlined + filled button pair, and count badges that read 0. On real sites only 13% of bars are light with a line. Logos are lettered wordmarks (75% are an SVG or image mark), not icon + text. Counts are hidden at 0. Over a third of bars are dark, lie over the opening image, or hide the links behind one Menu control. Build the bar assign.py drew (NEXT 3b) and letter the wordmark. (V4.8) The replacement habit is also a hit: a lettered name with an ornament stuck on, such as a coloured dot or full stop after it ("kept•", "grainline•"), a tick or apostrophe mark ("Link'light"), a bar or dashed perforation inside the word ("dock|et"), an underline under half the name, a superscript box, or the name split into two colours or two weights ("KettleLane**Books**", "Stripe*by*Stripe", "**Harbour** Notices"). On tool pages (operate) any row of text links in the top bar is a hit, including links to the app's own views: the bar holds the name and tool controls only, and views go in a rail or a tab row inside the work area.

The brief's own words always win: if it asks for one of these (a newspaper-style story, a dark terminal, a named font), follow it. A subject that feels warm, bookish, calm, friendly or child-facing is not such a request.

## 12. Pictogram catalogue (V4.12)
A grid of items where every picture is the same one-colour line drawing or pictogram (a house outline per hotel, a pot outline per product), usually on a large flat colour hero. It reads as a template; real catalogues show the thing. Draw each item as its own small scene with light and several colours (rule:atmospheric-imagery).
